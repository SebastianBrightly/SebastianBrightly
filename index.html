About me

My experience spans healthcare operations and technology, two environments that have shaped how I approach complex systems. Both have shown me that reliability is never just a technical concern. People, processes, constraints, and decisions all influence whether a system delivers what it was intended to deliver.

I tend to look beyond the immediate problem. I want to understand how the pieces fit together, where unnecessary complexity has accumulated, and what needs to change for things to work better as a whole. The newest technology isn't necessarily the right answer. The right answer is the one that addresses the underlying problem and holds up in the environment where it has to operate.

Good work should leave an organization more capable than it was before. That means clear ownership, knowledge that is shared rather than concentrated, and systems the next person can understand and build upon. The quality of an outcome matters. It should be able to stand long after the person who created it has moved on.

How I approach complex problems

Understand the system, not just the symptom

Before settling on a solution, I want to understand the underlying conditions.

  • What is driving the problem?
  • Who it affects?
  • What would a meaningful improvement look like?
The visible issue is not always the one that needs to be solved.

Think beyond the implementation

A solution has to do more than work in isolation. It needs to fit the environment, account for risk, and remain practical to operate and maintain.
I look for ways to make security, reliability, and good engineering habits part of how systems are built, rather than responsibilities added afterward.

Leave things better equipped for what comes next

I value work that creates lasting capability. Clear decisions, useful documentation, shared understanding, and deliberate ownership make it easier for teams to move forward without having to rediscover everything that came before.
A strong outcome should create momentum beyond the person who delivered it.

Curiosity keeps the work interesting

I'm drawn to problems that sit between disciplines, where technology, security, operations, and the way people work all influence one another.
I enjoy learning unfamiliar systems, questioning assumptions, and following a problem far enough to understand what's actually happening.

What I find most rewarding is connecting those pieces and finding a better way forward. Sometimes that means introducing something new. Sometimes it means simplifying what already exists, making a difficult process understandable, or helping people see a problem differently.
The value is in the improvement, beyond the novelty of the solution.

Good work starts with good conversations

I'm always interested in talking with people who enjoy thinking through difficult problems, challenging assumptions, and finding ways to make things work better. Whether the conversation is about technology, how teams approach their work, or an idea worth exploring, I'd be glad to connect.
